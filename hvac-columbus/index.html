When Your System Fails in Columbus

Call us with a problem and a real person answers, not a phone tree. The majority of calls get scheduled within a day, and true emergencies, an AC that quits in a heat wave or a furnace that fails on a freezing night, jump to the top of the schedule. Scheduling stays flexible so you're not left waiting for a callback.

The emergency calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. Year-round, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, clogged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Since our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these get fixed on the first trip, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and give us a call. Those few minutes can prevent a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Columbus

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

The age of your equipment plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the first heat wave of the year.

Minor issues rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a few miserable nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Columbus

Most local HVAC failures give warning signs before they happen. A breaker that trips more than once, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, a musty smell at startup. These are the early symptoms of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Left alone, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so small problems stay small.
